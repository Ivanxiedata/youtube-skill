# /yt-viral study: what is working in our niche (2026-11-21)

Weekly step "choosing what to make" (`docs/workflow.md`, section Weekly). Collected 2026-11-21 from public YouTube listings only.
It informs the titles and hooks of three episodes: what a coding agent is, Agent Skills, MCP going stateless.
Patterns only: nothing here is a premise, line, character or look to copy.

Method: `yt-dlp --flat-playlist -J --playlist-end 40 "https://www.youtube.com/@<handle>/videos"` (and `/shorts`), one channel at a time, no login, no cookies.
The 40 newest entries of each tab, entries without a view count dropped (9: IBM Technology 4, ByteByteGo 2, Matt Wolfe 3).
Then `python3 swipe.py collected.json --min 2.0` from `.claude/skills/yt-viral/`. Shorts are scored as their own series
("Fireship (Shorts)"), because a channel's Shorts and long videos have very different view distributions and one median would mix them.
Result: 681 videos, 10 channels, 19 series (10 long-form, 9 Shorts), 126 entries at 2x or better.
I did not watch any video; what I say about visual style comes from the search descriptions.
Raw listings, `collected.json` and the tool output are in the session scratchpad (`.../scratchpad/viral/`), not in the repo.

## What the top five share

The top five (rows 1 to 5 of the first table) each name one subject in plain words in the first few words of the title, then close with one small frame
that bounds the promise: a parenthetical exclusion, a parenthetical alternative, a step count, a "clearly explained" label, or a yes/no question.
All five are 60 characters or fewer, carry no hype adjective and put no creator or result in the title.
The same holds for the next entries on the long-form list (a subject plus a count of failure modes, a subject plus what changes for the viewer, a subject plus a count of examples).

Honest limit: this describes the winners, it does not separate them from the rest. Titles of outliers and non-outliers look alike on every surface measure I ran
(median 47 vs 49 characters on long videos; a digit in 36% vs 35%; a question mark in 20% vs 13%). Weak leans, small samples: "explained" appears in 6 of 64 long outliers
vs 9 of 327 others, and titles starting with what, why or how are 19 of 64 vs 48 of 327. The stronger signal is the subject: AI agents is in four of the first ten rows
(3, 4, 5, 10) and no other topic repeats like that.

## What the 60-second and Shorts entries do

- The listing gives no duration for Shorts, so I cannot say how many are about 60 seconds. The only long video under three minutes in the whole set is one 112-second ChatGPT how-to.
  Nobody in this set posts 60-second 16:9 explainers; the nearest long-form is the 5 to 6 minute explainer (ByteByteGo, Fireship, Two Minute Papers medians), and the nearest 60-second format is the vertical Short.
- Shorts titles are shorter: median 35 characters against 48 for long videos. One in five carries hashtags (ByteByteGo repeats the same hashtag string on many of its Shorts).
- Within Shorts, the title surface does not separate winners from the rest either (median 34.5 vs 35 characters; digit 26% vs 20%). What the on-niche winners have in common is the topic and a bounded size:
  a named concept with a step count or a time limit in the title, a pick-between-named-tools question, a single "thing to know" about one named tool, or a warning about a cost the viewer has now.
- The Shorts that beat their channel on The AI Advantage name a current model or feature (GPT-6 Astra, ChatGPT dots); I cannot tell from the listing how new those were.
- The non-AI Shorts that top the table: two are PC problems the viewer has today, stated as plainly as possible; the flying-car one is a curiosity with a plain subject and a parenthetical. Same plain, bounded shape, outside our niche.
- Shorts of the research channel (Two Minute Papers, 8 Shorts, none at 2x) do not win; the vertical format alone is not what works.

## For our three episodes

Brand constraints that apply to every pattern below (`docs/brand.md`, `docs/voice.md`): title 60 characters or fewer with the subject in the first 40 and a number, name or date;
no hype; the thumbnail already carries "LEARN <TOPIC> IN 60 SECONDS", so the title carries the hook; every number needs a source in `brief.json`.
This study has titles only, no transcripts, so nothing here says what the first 15 seconds should be; the repo rule stands (the first sentence confirms the title's subject and its frame).

Patterns found in the data (numbers refer to the long-form table unless "S" for the first table):

- **A. Subject, then one bounding frame.** The plain subject first, then a step count, a "clearly explained" label, a parenthetical, or a yes/no question (S rows 3, 4, 5; long-form 1, 2).
- **B. The old requirement still holding?** A yes/no question that sets the earlier way against the new one (long-form 2, 17.6x, on infrastructure).
- **C. A count of things that go wrong or to avoid** attached to the subject (long-form 4).
- **D. What it is, plus what changes for the viewer** in the same title (long-form 5).
- **E. The new thing measured against an earlier milestone** the viewer already knows (long-form 6).
- **F. The question people are already asking about a name they have heard** (ByteByteGo's MCP explainer, 4.62x).
- **G. Choosing between named tools** (Jeff Su Short, 8.36x; Shorts only so far).
- **H. A warning about something the viewer does now** (S row 2). Our voice rules forbid claims we cannot source and any hype, so use it only if the fact is true and sourced.

Which could be made this week with what we have: all three are already in progress, so the data only picks the frame. Cheapest to test: coding agents with pattern A or B,
because it is the topic with the most proof and the brand's side-by-side already sets old against new. Suggested use: write the 10 titles for each episode with at least two built on A and two on B, E or F, then score them with `title.py` as usual.

## Caveats

- The formula `swipe.py` names is a judgment about the title's words, matched by regular expressions against the 21 formulas in `yt-script/hooks.json`. It is not proof of why a video worked,
  and for 7 of the top 10 it names nothing. A title can sit on a video that won because of its thumbnail, a launch that week, a recommendation, or the channel's audience of that day.
- Public listings only: no login, no cookies, no account data. The listings give rounded view counts (so multiples are approximate), no upload dates, and only the 40 newest entries per tab.
  New uploads are still gathering views, which understates their multiple; the median is also taken over a window that mixes recent and older uploads, and no upload date lets me adjust for either.
- Shorts series have low medians and heavy tails; read any multiple above about 10x on a Shorts series as one lucky video, not a pattern.
- Off-niche items stay in the tool's ranking (rows 1, 6 and 8 of the first table) because the tool ranks by multiple only.
- The channel set is partly from my own knowledge of the niche; web search did not return a vetted list of short animated AI explainers, and none of the ten channels posts 60-second 16:9 videos.
- Other channels' titles are quoted here as data to study; the patterns above are descriptions, and our titles must be written from our own research, not from these.
