Inkling Two Mini: before, you'd stop and wait; your AI's written thinking cost you; now twelve layers loop three times, but how, why?
Here's how, in one hard exam question.
Before, an ordinary model, like Gemma or Kwen, ran each layer once per token. To think harder, it wrote its working out, line after line, and you waited.
Now, since November twentieth, Thinking Machines' Inkling Two Mini works it over in its head. Its middle twelve layers run three times, same weights: forty-eight layer steps from twenty-four layers, no bigger brain.
So it can write less. In the maker's test on a research twin, three passes wrote less than half the reasoning. And it's open, about twenty gigabytes, for a thirty-two gig Mac.
How? The middle layers loop. Why? More thinking per token, fewer tokens to wait for. The catch: it still writes some reasoning, and loops add no new facts.
Same exam; more working happens in its head.
