## Engine lessons (v2)
- The audit's "pop" check (mean frame difference over 0.2 on a 320x180 gray frame, above 6, off a cut) fires on: a scrolling text wall at close zoom (slow it to about 40 px/s), a big prop dropped fast (the binder at z 1.9 dropped 330 px: use 150 px, ease in and out, fade in), a whole screen squashed in under a second (fade it over 0.9 s instead) and a pan across a dense board faster than about 5 px a frame. Checking a frame range by rendering and differencing before the full build (a 40-line script) found them in a minute each; the build itself takes about 8 min.
- A hand IK target that crosses the shoulder's x at the same height flips the elbow (a 300 px pop); keep the hand on one side of the shoulder, use `tuck` for a folded arm, and ease any hand ramp over 0.8 s (a 0.35 s ramp over 285 px made a 41 px hand jump the audit flags).
- earshot `timeline` takes the most prominent onset near a cut, so a soft cut sound under running speech does not count, and a word onset 40-80 ms away reads "near". Plant the cut sound exactly on the cut (offset 0 reads OK; planting it 30 ms early reads -30 ms, on the edge of one frame).
- The mixer's soft limiter makes the true peak jump with effect gain (.40 gave -3.8 dBTP, .45 gave -1.1): re-measure after every gain change, `--audio-only` takes a minute.
- An earshot tag in a window is a mix reading, not a pointer to the newest effect: mix the stems separately (voice, music, effects; a 40-line scratch script with `audio_kit.Mix`) and run `earshot match "<tag>" --start --end` on each before changing a sound. Here the music was the cause. Run from zsh with `${=var}` if you word-split a variable.
- The corner tag (top-left, y 21-83, x 32-380) sits on the picture: a zoomed board shot puts text under it unless the camera y keeps the header's top at or below screen y 90. Screen position = (world - cam) * z + (960, 540).
