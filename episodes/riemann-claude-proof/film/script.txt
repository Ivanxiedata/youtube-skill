I know the world moves fast. So you probably missed a huge AI breakthrough last week!
A mathematician at Anthropic, working with Claude, released a proof of the Riemann hypothesis!

Riemann found special numbers that encode the primes, and predicted all the important ones sit on one line.
Earlier work showed at least two thirds of a key set follow it. But most isn't all: one exception would sink it.
A proof says every single one does.

And the proof? seventeen million lines of code! Far too long for a person to read. So how do we know it's right?
Two key checks. First: is it the right question? Two separate tools confirmed it proves exactly the standard statement.
Second: is every step right? It's written in Lean, a proof language whose checker checks every single step.
Several checkers by different people said yes; two are even proved unable to accept a false one, even if Lean's logic had a flaw. Certified correct!

What does it mean?
Certified doesn't mean understood. The checkers say it's true, not why. One mathematician says that could take months... or years.

That's the real change: checking and understanding have come apart. AI helped find it. Machines checked it. Mathematicians still have to understand it, to build on its ideas.

So, what we learned: a proven checker on the right question tells you it's right. Only understanding tells you why.
