# Lesson: gpt-6-2-cost-per-task

Main idea (one sentence, at most about 25 words: what the viewer will think differently; not the headline, not the mechanism):
An AI task costs price per token times the tokens it uses, so price per token alone can't tell you which model is cheaper. (24 words)

Why it matters (to the viewer or the field, with the research.md row):
You compare and pay for models by price per token, but what a task costs also depends on how many tokens the model uses for it: GPT-6.2 Astra's price per token is at least 20% below GPT-6 Astra's (row 5), yet on one work test, OpenAI says, it scored higher at under a third of the cost (row 3), which OpenAI credits to writing fewer tokens plus lower prices (rows 2, 7).
And the pricier model isn't always the better buy: on one coding test, both at max, OpenAI says the cheaper model, Sol, nearly matched Astra's score at about a fifth the cost (row 9). So you compare cost per task, which changes which model you pick.

Misconception it corrects:
"Price per token tells you the cost, and the pricier model is better." Said in the hook ("you pick by price") and line 3 ("you'd compare the rate: price per token, ... assuming pricier meant better"), corrected in the same breath of the hook ("but how much text it uses actually sets your cost") and by lines 4-6. Worded as the step the main idea changes (comparing, picking), not an end state (L89).

Before = comparing the rate ("you'd compare the rate: price per token"), when the rate alone can't tell the fare (S2); now = the meter, cost per task, set by the route (tokens used); the driver who picks the best route = the model that uses fewer tokens ("Like a driver who picks the best route"). Where it stops (`research.md`): more tokens can also be more thinking, not only a detour; an AI cost has input and output lines at different rates; these are OpenAI's results on its chosen tests; nobody is cheating.
